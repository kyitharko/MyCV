I am writing to apply for the Security Researcher position at Binarly. I hold a Ph.D. in Engineering from Kanazawa University, Japan, where my research focused on privacy and security in Named Data Networking. My doctoral work involved designing, implementing, and evaluating a privacy-preserving forwarding strategy based on Public Key Encryption with Keyword Search (PEKS). I implemented pairing-based cryptographic primitives in C++ using the PBC library, integrated them into the networking strategy, and benchmarked the resulting security and performance trade-offs. This research resulted in two peer-reviewed publications.

I am currently reimplementing this work in Rust using BLS12-381 and the arkworks ecosystem. Moving the original construction from symmetric to asymmetric pairings has required careful reasoning about group placement, pairing equations, implementation correctness, and protocol behavior. The implementation includes the core PEKS operations, RFC 9380 hash-to-curve functionality, and automated correctness tests.

My broader background includes Linux systems, computer networking, distributed systems, network security, packet-level analysis, network forensics, and system-level troubleshooting. While my primary research background is in cryptographic and network security rather than firmware reverse engineering, I am very interested in extending my systems and security research experience into vulnerability research, binary and program analysis, and software supply-chain security. I am comfortable working from technical papers and specifications, understanding unfamiliar systems in depth, and translating research concepts into working implementations.

I would welcome the opportunity to contribute my security research, cryptography, C++/Rust, and systems background to Binarly’s research team while continuing to deepen my expertise in low-level software security and vulnerability analysis.
